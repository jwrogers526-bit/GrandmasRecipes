Slow-Smoked BBQ Brisket & Mac

Low, slow, smoky—and worth every minute.

A tender barbecue brisket paired with a bubbling pan of baked macaroni and cheese. Two Southern favorites sharing one supper table.

Prep time35 minutes
Cook time10–14+ hours
Servings8–10
Jump to the recipe ↓All recipes
Part one of two

The slow-smoked brisket.

Start early: brisket takes most of the day, and its tenderness matters more than the clock.

What you'll need

  • 1 whole beef brisket, about 8–10 lb, trimmed
  • 3 tbsp coarse kosher salt
  • 3 tbsp coarse black pepper
  • 1 tbsp garlic powder
  • 1 tbsp smoked paprika, optional
  • Wood chunks or pellets (oak or hickory)
  • Butcher paper or heavy foil for wrapping

Let's smoke it

  1. Season: Pat the brisket dry and coat with the salt, pepper, garlic powder, and paprika.
  2. Heat the smoker: Preheat to 225–250°F. Place brisket fat side toward the main heat source.
  3. Smoke low and slow: Cook until the bark looks deep brown and the internal temperature is around 160–170°F, often after several hours.
  4. Wrap: Wrap brisket tightly in butcher paper or foil. Return it to the smoker and continue cooking until a probe slides in with little resistance, often around 195–205°F internally.
  5. Rest safely: Remove from heat and rest, wrapped, for about 45–60 minutes before slicing. If resting longer, keep it safely hot at 140°F or above.
  6. Slice and serve: Cut against the grain into tender slices. Save a little of the juices for spooning over the meat.
Part two of two

The baked mac & cheese.

While the brisket rests, let the oven handle the creamy, golden side dish.

What you'll need

  • 1 lb elbow macaroni
  • 4 tbsp butter
  • ¼ cup all-purpose flour
  • 3 cups whole milk, warmed
  • 2 cups shredded sharp cheddar
  • 1½ cups shredded Monterey Jack
  • ½ tsp mustard powder
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • ½ cup panko breadcrumbs and 1 tbsp melted butter, optional topping

Let's bake it

  1. Prepare: Heat oven to 350°F. Grease a 9-by-13-inch baking dish. Cook macaroni for 1–2 minutes less than the package says; drain.
  2. Start the sauce: Melt 4 tbsp butter over medium heat. Stir in flour and cook about 1 minute. Slowly whisk in warm milk until smooth and slightly thickened.
  3. Add the cheese: Remove from direct heat. Stir in most of the cheddar and Jack, plus mustard powder, paprika, salt, and pepper. Reserve a handful of cheese for the top.
  4. Combine: Fold cooked pasta into the sauce and transfer to the baking dish. Sprinkle remaining cheese on top.
  5. Make it golden: For a crunchy topping, mix breadcrumbs with 1 tbsp melted butter and scatter over the top.
  6. Bake: Bake about 20–25 minutes until bubbling and lightly browned. Rest 5 minutes before serving.

Grandma's kitchen tip

Brisket is done when it feels tender, not when a timer rings. Start the mac and cheese near the end of the brisket cook so they reach the table warm together. If you don't own a smoker, a covered low oven can make tender brisket, though it won't taste quite as smoky.

Save room for seconds.
